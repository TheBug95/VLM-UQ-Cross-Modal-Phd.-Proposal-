# D06 — Training-Free Uncertainty Estimation Baselines for Cross-Modal KL Divergence

> Research dimension: Deep-dive investigation into post-hoc, training-free uncertainty estimation methods that can serve as baselines for a cross-modal KL divergence approach using a frozen MedGemma VLM.  
> Target: ≥3000 words with structured citations [^id].  
> Date compiled: 2026-07-22.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This document surveys **training-free (post-hoc) uncertainty estimation methods** suitable as baselines for a proposed cross-modal KL divergence approach. The proposal uses a frozen vision-language model (MedGemma) and derives uncertainty from the divergence between vision and text feature distributions. All baselines reviewed here require **no fine-tuning of the VLM** and are ideally **single-pass** (no ensemble or repeated sampling). The findings are organized into ten categories: output-based scores, hidden-state probing, MC-Dropout approximations, test-time augmentation, semantic entropy, energy-based methods, calibration techniques, comparison benchmarks, open-source libraries, and intrinsic limitations of each family.

---

## 2. Output-Based Baselines

### 2.1 Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP)

**Claim:** MSP is the simplest and most widely used training-free uncertainty signal. It uses the maximum predicted class probability as a confidence proxy; lower MSP implies higher uncertainty. [^1][^2]

**Source:** Multiple survey and benchmark papers (ViLU, FUSE, Out-of-Distribution Detection surveys).

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07620v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14728v1

**Date:** 2025, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "MCM estimates uncertainty in VLMs by leveraging the softmax probability distribution over all classes or captions. It selects the most likely caption for an image based on the highest probability score, providing a natural measure of confidence in the model's predictions. No additional training is required." [^1]

**Context:** For classification, MSP works well as a first-line baseline. For VQA, it requires casting the problem as multiple-choice (first-token selection) or aggregating token probabilities, which is a common simplification in VLM uncertainty benchmarks. [^2]

**Confidence:** High — MSP is universally implemented and validated.

---

### 2.2 Predictive Entropy and Shannon Entropy

**Claim:** Entropy of the output probability distribution measures uncertainty by quantifying how dispersed the probability mass is. High entropy → uncertainty; low entropy → confidence. It is training-free and operates directly on softmax outputs. [^3][^4]

**Source:** ViLU paper; HyperMix OOD detection paper; EMNLP surveys.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07620v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15086v1

**Date:** 2025, 2023

**Excerpt:**
> "Entropy-based uncertainty estimation does not require additional training. High entropy indicates more significant uncertainty, as the model assigns similar probabilities across multiple classes, reflecting ambiguity in its prediction." [^3]

**Context:** In VLM zero-shot classification, entropy is typically computed over the similarity scores between image and text prompts. It captures *aleatoric* uncertainty (data ambiguity) but not *epistemic* uncertainty (model ignorance). It is a standard baseline in nearly every VLM uncertainty paper. [^4]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 2.3 Margin (Top-2 Confidence Difference)

**Claim:** The margin between the top two predicted class probabilities is a simple discriminative signal. Small margins indicate ambiguous decisions. It is mentioned in classification baselines but less commonly for VLMs. [^5]

**Source:** General ML calibration literature; OOD detection surveys.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10261v2

**Date:** 2023

**Excerpt:**
> Standard softmax baselines include MSP, Entropy, and margin-based scores for confidence estimation.

**Context:** Margin is rarely used as a standalone VLM baseline because VLMs do not have a fixed class hierarchy; however, it can be adapted by ranking text prompt similarities.

**Confidence:** Medium — works for classification, less natural for open-ended VQA.

---

### 2.4 Temperature Scaling (TS)

**Claim:** Temperature scaling is a single-parameter post-hoc calibration method that rescales logits before softmax by a learned scalar T > 0. It significantly improves ECE for VLMs without changing model weights. T is fit on a small validation set via LBFGS to minimize NLL. [^6][^7]

**Source:** "An Empirical Study Into What Matters for Calibrating Vision-Language Models" (ICML 2024); MedBayes-Lite.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07417v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16625v2

**Date:** 2024, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Our findings reveal that while VLMs are not inherently calibrated for uncertainty, temperature scaling significantly and consistently improves calibration, even across shifts in distribution and changes in label set. Moreover, VLMs can be calibrated with a very small set of examples." [^6]

**Context:** For a frozen MedGemma, TS is attractive because it only requires a small held-out set of (question, image, answer) triples to learn T. It preserves rankings and adds negligible inference cost. It is often stacked *on top of* other uncertainty methods (e.g., UAT-Lite + TS). [^7]

**Confidence:** Very High — the de-facto standard for VLM calibration.

## 3. Hidden-State Probing

### 3.1 Azaria & Mitchell (2023) — SAPLMA

**Claim:** SAPLMA demonstrated that a lightweight classifier (MLP probe) trained on hidden layer activations can predict the truthfulness of LLM statements with 60–80% accuracy, outperforming output-probability methods. The probe is trained *post-hoc* on a labeled set of correct/incorrect model outputs. [^8][^9]

**Source:** SAPLMA paper; "Do LLMs Really Know What They Don't Know?" survey.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02784v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09033v2

**Date:** 2023, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Azaria and Mitchell (2023) demonstrated that hidden states contain veracity signals, enabling efficient detection. Classifiers trained on these internal activations outperform methods based on output probabilities, which are confounded by factors such as sentence length and token frequency." [^8]

**Context:** For VLMs, the same principle applies: vision encoder hidden states, cross-attention maps, or decoder hidden states can be probed. However, the probe requires **training data** (labeled correct/incorrect examples). Whether this counts as "training-free" depends on the strictness of the definition — the *VLM* is frozen, but the *probe* is not. [^9]

**Confidence:** High for LLMs; Medium-to-High for VLMs (cross-modal probing is less explored).

---

### 3.2 Orgad et al. (2025) — Token-Level Truthfulness Signals

**Claim:** Orgad et al. showed that internal representations encode *token-level* truthfulness signals concentrated at "exact answer" token positions. Probes at these positions outperform last-token probes, but locating the exact answer token requires ground-truth knowledge (a chicken-and-egg problem). [^10][^11]

**Source:** Multiple 2025–2026 hallucination detection papers citing Orgad.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05025v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26366v3

**Date:** 2025, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Orgad et al. (2025) evaluate multiple token positions—including the last generated token, the last prompt token, and the 'exact answer' token—and found that representations at the exact-answer position achieve the best downstream performance within the hidden-state probing framework." [^10]

**Context:** For VQA, the "answer token" is often the first generated token after the prompt. If the task is framed as multiple-choice, the position is fixed. For open-ended VQA, identifying the answer span requires auxiliary parsing. The probe is small (logistic regression or 2–3 layer MLP), so training cost is minimal. [^11]

**Confidence:** Medium — position dependency complicates deployment.

---

### 3.3 Is Probing "Training-Free"?

**Claim:** The VLM itself remains frozen, but the probe is a small supervised model trained on hidden states extracted from a labeled dataset. In the literature, this is often termed **post-hoc** or **white-box** rather than strictly training-free. [^12][^13]

**Source:** EnsemHalDet paper; Between the Layers paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02784v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22299v1

**Date:** 2026, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Internal-representation-based approaches are based on the premise that a model's internal states encode the truthfulness of its outputs. Most existing methods rely on a single internal representation or a single detector, which may limit their ability to capture the diverse characteristics of hallucinations." [^12]

**Context:** For the proposal, a hidden-state probe can be viewed as a **comparable baseline** rather than a strictly training-free one. If the probe is small (e.g., linear or 2-layer MLP) and trained on a few hundred examples, it is lightweight but not zero-training. [^13]

**Confidence:** High — consensus in literature: probe training is separate from VLM training.

## 4. MC-Dropout Alternatives & Single-Pass Approximations

### 4.1 Standard MC-Dropout

**Claim:** MC-Dropout retains dropout at test time and performs multiple stochastic forward passes to approximate Bayesian inference. It is effective but multi-pass, making it expensive for large VLMs. [^14][^15]

**Source:** UAT-Lite paper; EmergentMind MC-Dropout survey.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02952v2, https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/monte-carlo-dropout-mc-dropout

**Date:** 2026, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "MC dropout provides a computationally efficient approximation to Bayesian inference... However, the additional computational work involved in the multiple forward passes of the same input can be prohibitively expensive and thus make this method too slow for deployment." [^14]

**Context:** For a frozen MedGemma, MC-Dropout requires the model to have dropout layers (many modern VLMs do not, or use dropout only in specific submodules). If dropout is unavailable, MC-Dropout cannot be applied without architectural modification, which violates the training-free constraint. [^15]

**Confidence:** High — well understood; limited applicability to frozen proprietary VLMs.

---

### 4.2 Single-Pass Moment Propagation (MP)

**Claim:** A single-shot approximation to MC-Dropout propagates the mean and variance of activations through the network analytically, yielding uncertainty in one forward pass. It achieves time savings proportional to the number of MC samples (e.g., 10,000×). [^16][^17]

**Source:** UDL 2020 paper; Portuguese thesis on MC-Dropout approximations.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03293.pdf, https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/bitstream/10216/156500/2/655724.pdf

**Date:** 2020, 2022

**Excerpt:**
> "With our MP approach we have introduced an approximation to MC dropout which requires no sampling but instead propagates the expectation and the variance of the signal through the network. This results in a time saving by a factor that approximately corresponds to the number of MC runs." [^16]

**Context:** Moment propagation has been demonstrated for regression and feedforward networks. Extension to transformer-based VLMs is non-trivial due to attention mechanisms and layer normalization, but represents a promising research direction for single-pass epistemic uncertainty. [^17]

**Confidence:** Medium — promising for simple architectures; unproven for VLMs.

---

### 4.3 DropConnect and Component-Specific Dropout

**Claim:** MC-DropConnect outperforms MC-Dropout in some medical imaging tasks, achieving lower prediction error with fewer samples (2 vs. 6). However, it still requires stochastic forward passes. [^18]

**Source:** DropConnect Is Effective paper; UAT-Lite paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.04569.pdf, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02952v2

**Date:** 2019, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "MC-DropConnect results in a significantly lower prediction error than the baseline network after only 2 samples while this number is 6 for MC-Dropout." [^18]

**Context:** Component-specific dropout rates (0.1 for embeddings, 0.2 for attention, 0.3 for FFN) have been proposed to balance variability and stability. For VLMs, this would require access to internal dropout masks, which may not be available in frozen models. [^14]

**Confidence:** Medium — requires architectural access.

## 5. Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)

### 5.1 TTA as Uncertainty Baseline

**Claim:** TTA applies deterministic transformations (flip, rotation, noise, crop) to the input image at test time, runs the model on each augmented variant, and aggregates predictions. The variance across augmented predictions quantifies aleatoric uncertainty. It requires no model modification or retraining. [^19][^20]

**Source:** SAIL paper; Politecnico di Milano thesis on TTA for medical imaging.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02671v1, https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/2174614b-1652-473e-9ef1-aebe8c041e74/2025_07_Mondo_Lorenzo_Tesi_01.pdf

**Date:** 2025, 2025

**Excerpt:**
> "Test-Time Augmentation methods produce uncertainty estimations by applying data augmentations to input samples during inference, generating multiple predictions per input instead of a single deterministic output. By analyzing the variance among augmented predictions, TTA provides a computationally efficient estimation on aleatoric uncertainty, without requiring modifications to model architecture or training procedures." [^19]

**Context:** For VLMs, TTA can be applied to the *vision* input while keeping the text prompt fixed. However, text augmentation is harder (paraphrasing changes semantics). TTA is naturally multi-pass, so it is not single-pass. Computational cost scales linearly with the number of augmentations. For clinical workflows, TTA can be expensive but remains cheaper than ensemble training. [^20]

**Confidence:** High — established method; cost is the main concern.

---

### 5.2 TTA for VLM: Efficiency Considerations

**Claim:** In VLM test-time adaptation literature, augmentation-dependent methods (e.g., TPT, MEMO) are among the slowest due to multiple forward passes per sample. Training-free methods (e.g., TDA, ZERO) avoid augmentation and are much faster. [^21]

**Source:** SAIL paper efficiency analysis; SubTTA paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02671v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08139v1

**Date:** 2025, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "TPT performs prompt tuning with gradient backward and multiple augmentations, making it the slowest method overall; TDA significantly improves efficiency with a training-free, cache-based strategy." [^21]

**Context:** If the proposal requires a **single-pass** baseline, TTA may not qualify unless the number of augmentations is very small (e.g., 2–3 flips). Alternatively, TTA can serve as a non-single-pass upper bound for comparison.

**Confidence:** High.

## 6. Semantic Entropy and p(True)

### 6.1 Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al.)

**Claim:** Semantic entropy clusters multiple generated responses by semantic equivalence (via entailment) and computes entropy over *meaning clusters* rather than individual token sequences. It addresses the limitation that raw entropy is confounded by lexical diversity. [^22][^23]

**Source:** FUSE paper; SUGAR paper; Semantic Entropy survey.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14728v1, https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04899

**Date:** 2026, 2025

**Excerpt:**
> "Semantic entropy measures the uncertainty over different meanings. First, 10 potential high-temperature answers are generated, then bidirectional entailment is used to detect varying forms of one meaning among these generations. Lastly, the likelihood of generating each of the semantic clusters is computed." [^22]

**Context:** For VQA, semantic entropy requires generating multiple answers per (image, question) pair and clustering them. This is **not single-pass** and requires an entailment model (e.g., DeBERTa). However, it is training-free w.r.t. the VLM and is highly effective at detecting hallucinations in generative models. [^23]

**Confidence:** High — state-of-the-art for hallucination detection in NLG/VQA.

---

### 6.2 p(True) / Verbalized Confidence

**Claim:** p(True) prompts the model to assess whether its own answer is true. It is a black-box method requiring no internal access. However, models are systematically overconfident, making verbalized confidence poorly calibrated. [^24][^25]

**Source:** SCOPE paper; D2HScore paper; HalluGuard survey.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13110v3, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11569v1

**Date:** 2026, 2025

**Excerpt:**
> "Verbalized Confidence (VC) prompts the LLM to report its own confidence in its generated output. However, VC-based approaches are limited by the tendency of LLMs to be overconfident, often assigning high self-confidence scores even to incorrect or hallucinated outputs." [^24]

**Context:** For VLMs, p(True) can be adapted by asking the model "Is this answer correct?" after generation. It is very cheap (single extra forward pass or even zero if formatted as a single prompt) but has been shown to be unreliable, especially after RLHF tuning. [^25]

**Confidence:** High — widely studied; known to be miscalibrated.

---

### 6.3 Length-Normalized Entropy (LN-Entropy)

**Claim:** LN-Entropy normalizes the joint log-probability of a generated sequence by its length, making it suitable for comparing sequences of different lengths. It is a standard baseline for VLM/VQA uncertainty. [^26]

**Source:** FUSE paper; LEC paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14728v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01556v3

**Date:** 2026, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "LN-Entropy normalizes the joint log-probability of each sequence by dividing it by the sequence length and is proposed for uncertainty quantification in LLM." [^26]

**Context:** LN-Entropy is a simple correction to raw sequence perplexity and is applicable to any autoregressive VLM. It does not require multiple samples (single-pass) but only captures model confidence, not semantic diversity.

**Confidence:** High.

## 7. Energy-Based Methods

### 7.1 Energy Score (Liu et al. 2020)

**Claim:** The energy score computes the log-sum-exp of logits: E(x) = log Σ exp(f_i(x)). Lower energy corresponds to higher confidence (in-distribution). It is a single-pass, training-free score that often outperforms MSP for OOD detection. [^27][^28]

**Source:** "How Good Are LLMs at OOD Detection?" paper; OOD survey (ACM 2025).

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10261v2, https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3760390

**Date:** 2023, 2025

**Excerpt:**
> "Energy score is inspired by energy-based models, which computes confidence score as S(x) = log Σ_i exp((w^T · z)_i). Note that for both MSP and Energy, we only select the probability and logits corresponding to the first token of each class name." [^27]

**Context:** For VLMs, energy can be computed over the similarity logits between the image and all text prompts. It is a natural alternative to MSP because it uses the *entire* logit distribution rather than just the maximum. It has been shown to be superior for OOD detection in both vision and language models. [^28]

**Confidence:** High — strong empirical performance; simple to implement.

---

### 7.2 Mahalanobis Distance (Lee et al. 2018)

**Claim:** Mahalanobis distance fits class-conditional Gaussian distributions to penultimate-layer features and measures the distance of a test sample to these distributions. A variant, Relative Mahalanobis Distance (RMD), improves near-OOD detection by normalizing against a background model. [^29][^30]

**Source:** OOD detection papers; LAPT paper; Relative Mahalanobis paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10261v2, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.09022

**Date:** 2023, 2021

**Excerpt:**
> "Mahalanobis distance fits K-class conditional Gaussian distributions for the K in-distribution classes, based on the output z obtained from the penultimate layer of the model. The performance of MD improves significantly after model fine-tuning." [^29]

**Context:** For VLMs, Mahalanobis can be applied in the *joint* embedding space (vision or text features). However, it requires feature statistics (mean/covariance) computed on a reference dataset. This is training-free w.r.t. the VLM but requires a representative calibration set. Relative Mahalanobis (RMD) is hyperparameter-free and improves near-OOD detection. [^30]

**Confidence:** High for OOD; Medium for general uncertainty — requires careful feature extraction.

---

### 7.3 Cosine Distance / KNN in Embedding Space

**Claim:** Cosine similarity between the test input embedding and the nearest validation-set embedding provides a simple distance-based uncertainty score. KNN-based methods extend this to k-nearest neighbors. [^31]

**Source:** OOD detection paper; Intra-Class Probabilistic Embeddings paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10261v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22019

**Date:** 2023, 2025

**Excerpt:**
> "Cosine distance confidence score is defined as the highest cosine similarity between the test input representation and the representations in the validation set." [^31]

**Context:** For a frozen VLM, cosine distance can be computed between the image embedding and the embeddings of the training/calibration set. It captures distributional typicality — how "familiar" the input is. This is a natural complement to cross-modal KL divergence, which measures *alignment* rather than *typicality*. [^31]

**Confidence:** High.

## 8. Calibration Methods

### 8.1 Platt Scaling

**Claim:** Platt scaling fits a logistic regression (sigmoid mapping) from raw scores to calibrated probabilities. It is data-efficient and works well for binary tasks. For VQA, it can be applied to the score of the predicted answer token. [^32][^33]

**Source:** Kdnuggets calibration tutorial; Platt scaling paper.

**URL:** https://www.kdnuggets.com/a-deep-dive-into-calibration-of-language-models-platt-scaling-isotonic-regression-temperature-scaling, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00326

**Date:** 2026, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Platt scaling fits a logistic function over the uncalibrated scores: p = σ(A·s + B), where A and B are learned from a held-out validation set with binary correctness labels. The two-parameter fit is data-efficient compared to isotonic regression." [^32]

**Context:** For a VLM answering multiple-choice VQA, Platt scaling can calibrate the probability of the selected answer. It requires fewer samples than isotonic regression and adds negligible inference overhead. However, it assumes a roughly sigmoid calibration curve. [^33]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 8.2 Isotonic Regression

**Claim:** Isotonic regression is a non-parametric calibration method that fits a monotonically increasing piecewise function. It is more flexible than Platt but requires more calibration data and can overfit. [^34]

**Source:** MedBayes-Lite paper; UQ4ML tutorial.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16625v2, https://github.com/rizbicki/UQ4ML/blob/main/notebooks/Classification_Calibration_Notebook.md

**Date:** 2026, 2024

**Excerpt:**
> "Isotonic Regression provides a non-parametric mapping between predicted confidence and empirical accuracy and is implemented using scikit-learn's monotone regression with clipping for out-of-range predictions." [^34]

**Context:** Isotonic regression is suitable when the calibration curve is non-sigmoid (e.g., U-shaped). For VLMs with complex miscalibration patterns, it may outperform Platt if sufficient calibration data is available. [^34]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 8.3 Temperature Scaling vs. Platt vs. Isotonic

**Claim:** Temperature scaling is the simplest (one parameter, no binning) and most commonly used for deep networks. Platt is better for binary or heavily skewed outputs. Isotonic is the most flexible but data-hungry. For VLMs, TS dominates in the literature. [^35]

**Source:** "An Empirical Study Into What Matters for Calibrating VLMs"; Kdnuggets article.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07417v1, https://www.kdnuggets.com/a-deep-dive-into-calibration-of-language-models-platt-scaling-isotonic-regression-temperature-scaling

**Date:** 2024, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Temperature scaling is a simple one-parameter variant of Platt scaling that has become a standard neural-network calibration baseline. In the VLM setting, recent work shows that post-hoc or prompt-aware methods can improve calibration under shifts in distribution, label space, or prompt adaptation." [^35]

**Context:** For the proposed cross-modal KL method, stacking TS on top of the KL-derived score (to calibrate it) is a natural and fair baseline comparison. The proposal should compare raw KL score, KL+TS, and raw TS alone. [^35]

**Confidence:** Very High.

## 9. Comparison Papers, Benchmarks, and Surveys

### 9.1 VLM Uncertainty Benchmarks

**Claim:** Several 2024–2026 papers benchmark uncertainty methods for VLMs. Key findings: (1) Accuracy and uncertainty are not aligned; high-accuracy models can be highly uncertain. (2) Conformal prediction provides statistically valid uncertainty scores. (3) ECE and AUROC are standard metrics. [^36][^37]

**Source:** Uncertainty-Aware Evaluation for VLMs (Kostumov et al.); FUSE paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14418, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14728v1

**Date:** 2024, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Our analysis spans 20+ VLMs, focusing on the multiple-choice VQA task. We examine models on 5 datasets. Using conformal prediction as an uncertainty estimation approach, we demonstrate that the models' uncertainty is not aligned with their accuracy." [^36]

**Context:** These benchmarks confirm that no single uncertainty method dominates across all VLM architectures and datasets. A cross-modal KL divergence method should be evaluated on the same benchmark suite (e.g., VQAv2, OKVQA, AdVQA) for fair comparison. [^37]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 9.2 Training-Free vs. Training-Based Methods

**Claim:** Training-free methods (MCM, entropy, TS, TTA, energy) are faster but generally less accurate than methods that train small adapters or probes (ViLU, ProbVLM, PCME++). The trade-off is between deployment cost and detection performance. [^38][^39]

**Source:** ViLU paper; Intra-Class Probabilistic Embeddings paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07620v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22019

**Date:** 2025, 2025

**Excerpt:**
> "PCME, PCME++, and ProbVLM use probabilistic adapters to model distributions over text and image embeddings, but they require finetuning on a dataset for a specific task. Our method leverages class-wise distribution modeling over frozen VLM features to assess the distributional typicality of predictions, achieving effective uncertainty estimation without retraining or architectural modification." [^38]

**Context:** The proposal's cross-modal KL divergence is a training-free method (no VLM fine-tuning). It should be compared against other training-free baselines (MCM, entropy, TS, energy, TTA) and optionally against lightweight trained methods (e.g., a small probe) to show the value of avoiding training. [^39]

**Confidence:** High.

## 10. Open-Source Libraries and Code Availability

### 10.1 Torch-Uncertainty

**Claim:** Torch-Uncertainty is a PyTorch/Lightning-based open-source framework that implements Deep Ensembles, MC-Dropout, Packed Ensembles, Test-Time Adaptation (Zero), Temperature Scaling, Isotonic Regression, Conformal Prediction, and many metrics (ECE, AUROC, Brier). It supports classification, regression, and segmentation. [^40][^41]

**Source:** Torch-Uncertainty paper (2025); GitHub repository.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10282v1, https://github.com/torch-uncertainty/torch-uncertainty

**Date:** 2025, 2023

**Excerpt:**
> "Torch-Uncertainty offers a unified and extensible architecture that significantly reduces the engineering overhead typically associated with implementing uncertainty-aware models and evaluating their performance on out-of-distribution detection, distribution shift, and selective classification." [^40]

**Context:** For the proposal, Torch-Uncertainty can provide ready-to-use implementations of TS, isotonic regression, MC-Dropout, and evaluation metrics. It does not include VLM-specific methods (e.g., MCM, semantic entropy) but can be extended. [^41]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 10.2 Uncertainty Toolbox

**Claim:** Uncertainty Toolbox (Python) focuses on regression tasks, providing calibration plots, interval scores, and metrics. It is lightweight and easy to integrate. [^42]

**Source:** Helmholtz AI presentation; Uncertainty Toolbox paper.

**URL:** https://indico.desy.de/event/46718/contributions/178183/attachments/93744/127325/main.pdf, https://github.com/uncertainty-toolbox/uncertainty-toolbox

**Date:** 2024, 2021

**Excerpt:**
> "Uncertainty Toolbox: An Open-Source Library for Assessing, Visualizing, and Improving Uncertainty Quantification." [^42]

**Context:** Primarily for regression; less relevant for VLM classification/VQA but useful for ablation studies if the proposal includes regression subtasks (e.g., severity scoring). [^42]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 10.3 Laplace Library (laplace-torch)

**Claim:** laplace-torch provides last-layer Laplace approximations for Bayesian neural networks. It is used in recent papers as a baseline for uncertainty estimation but requires gradients and is not single-pass. [^43]

**Source:** Tubular Riemannian Laplace paper; BLoB paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24381v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11675v1

**Date:** 2025, 2024

**Excerpt:**
> "We utilize the laplace-torch library for standard Laplace approximations. To handle the high dimensionality of ResNet-18, we employ the Last-Layer approximation, treating the feature extractor as fixed and inferring the posterior only over the weights of the final classification layer." [^43]

**Context:** Laplace approximations are not training-free in the strict sense (they require posterior fitting over weights). For a frozen VLM, last-layer Laplace is feasible but requires gradient access to the final layer, which may not be available for all model APIs. [^43]

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Confidence:** High.

## 11. Intrinsic Limitations of Each Baseline Family

### 11.1 Why Entropy/MSP Fails for High-Confidence Hallucinations

**Claim:** Output-based methods (entropy, MSP) fail when models are *confidently wrong* — i.e., the probability distribution is sharply peaked around an incorrect answer. In these cases, entropy is low and MSP is high, giving a false signal of certainty. This is a well-documented phenomenon in LLMs and VLMs, especially after RLHF. [^45][^46]

**Source:** Hallucination Detection and Mitigation paper; D2HScore paper; BaseCal paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09929v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11569v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03042v1

**Date:** 2025, 2025, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Traditional uncertainty measures, such as token-level entropy and semantic entropy, are effective at identifying hallucinations where the model exhibits internal uncertainty. However, these measures fail completely when LLMs and LRMs produce high-confidence hallucinations, where the model consistently generates the same incorrect output with high certainty. In such cases, entropy remains low because the model's probability distribution is sharply peaked around wrong answers." [^45]

**Context:** This is a **critical limitation** for medical VQA, where a VLM may confidently misdiagnose a rare condition because the training data distribution is biased. The proposed cross-modal KL divergence may detect this if the vision and text features are misaligned even when the text output is confident. [^46]

**Confidence:** Very High.

---

### 11.2 Why Probing Fails Under Distribution Shift

**Claim:** Hidden-state probes capture *task-specific* correlations rather than a global truthfulness signal. They struggle to generalize across datasets, topics, and under distribution shift because the geometry of hidden states changes with input domain. [^47][^48]

**Source:** Detecting Hallucinations via Internal Attention Divergence paper; "Do LLMs Really Know..." paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05025v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09033v2

**Date:** 2026, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Probes trained on hidden states tend to capture task-specific correlations rather than a global confidence signal. Representation-based signals struggle to distinguish associated hallucinations from factual associations but can more effectively separate unassociated hallucinations from factual associations." [^47]

**Context:** For a medical VLM deployed across different hospitals (different scanners, demographics), a probe trained on one dataset may degrade. The proposed cross-modal KL divergence, if it relies on *relative* divergence rather than absolute feature values, may be more robust to domain shift. [^48]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 11.3 Why MC-Dropout Is Limited for Modern VLMs

**Claim:** Many modern VLMs (especially those without explicit dropout in inference, or with API-only access) do not support MC-Dropout. Additionally, even when supported, the uncertainty estimates depend heavily on dropout rate and placement, often more than on data. [^49]

**Source:** EmergentMind MC-Dropout survey; MC-Dropout limitations paper.

**URL:** https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/monte-carlo-dropout-mc-dropout, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03293.pdf

**Date:** 2026, 2020

**Excerpt:**
> "Uncertainty estimates are often more influenced by dropout rate, placement, and network width than data. In finite, trained networks, output distributions depart from Gaussian behavior due to correlations, with heavy-tailed or skewed marginals emerging." [^49]

**Context:** If MedGemma does not expose dropout masks or uses a different regularization strategy, MC-Dropout is inapplicable. This makes training-free alternatives (energy, KL divergence, TTA) more practical. [^49]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 11.4 Why TTA Has Limited Scope for VQA

**Claim:** TTA is primarily applicable to the *vision* modality. For VQA, augmenting text questions (paraphrasing) risks changing semantics, so TTA mainly tests robustness to image perturbations. It does not capture uncertainty in the reasoning chain. [^50]

**Source:** SAIL paper; TTA thesis.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02671v1, https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/2174614b-1652-473e-9ef1-aebe8c041e74/2025_07_Mondo_Lorenzo_Tesi_01.pdf

**Date:** 2025, 2025

**Excerpt:**
> "Test-Time Augmentation methods produce uncertainty estimations by applying data augmentations to input samples during inference. It is however mainly applicable in computer vision." [^50]

**Context:** For medical images, standard augmentations (flip, rotation, intensity) may be clinically meaningful (simulating scanner variability), but the uncertainty signal is only about *image* robustness, not about *cross-modal* alignment. [^50]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 11.5 Why Semantic Entropy Is Expensive

**Claim:** Semantic entropy requires multiple sampled generations + entailment clustering. This makes it multi-pass and computationally expensive, though it is the strongest baseline for hallucination detection in generative tasks. [^51]

**Source:** FUSE paper; Kuhn et al. original paper.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14728v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07253v2

**Date:** 2026, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Semantic Entropy models the uncertainty over different meanings by clustering the generated sequences and measuring the clusters' entropies. We set the temperature to 0.5 and the sample size to three." [^51]

**Context:** For a single-pass baseline comparison, semantic entropy is not a direct competitor. However, it should be included as a "strong but expensive" upper bound in the benchmark. [^51]

**Confidence:** High.

---

### 11.6 Why Calibration Methods Do Not Fix Miscalibration at the Source

**Claim:** Post-hoc calibration (TS, Platt, isotonic) adjusts confidence scores to match empirical accuracy but does not change the model's internal reasoning. They cannot detect *why* a prediction is wrong — only *how confidently* it is expressed. Under severe distribution shift, calibration itself degrades. [^52]

**Source:** BaseCal paper; Kdnuggets calibration article; MedBayes-Lite.

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03042v1, https://www.kdnuggets.com/a-deep-dive-into-calibration-of-language-models-platt-scaling-isotonic-regression-temperature-scaling, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16625v2

**Date:** 2026, 2026, 2026

**Excerpt:**
> "Post-trained language models exhibit significant overconfidence, often assigning high confidence even to incorrect responses. Supervised calibration methods rely heavily on human-labeled data, which is often difficult to obtain in real-world applications." [^52]

**Context:** The proposed cross-modal KL divergence is a *feature-based* uncertainty signal, not a *score-based* one. It may detect misalignment even when the output probability is well-calibrated, offering a complementary failure mode. [^52]

**Confidence:** High.

## 12. Synthesis: Recommended Baseline Suite for Cross-Modal KL Divergence

Based on the survey, the following baseline suite is recommended for comparing the proposed cross-modal KL divergence method on a frozen MedGemma VLM for medical VQA:

| Baseline | Category | Training-Free? | Single-Pass? | Cost | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP)** | Output | Yes | Yes | Free | Simple, universally applicable | Fails on confident errors |
| **Predictive Entropy** | Output | Yes | Yes | Free | Captures ambiguity | Fails on confident errors |
| **Temperature Scaling (TS)** | Calibration | Yes (post-hoc fit) | Yes | Minimal | Best calibration improvement | Needs small calibration set; no causal insight |
| **Energy Score** | Energy | Yes | Yes | Free | Uses full logit distribution | Less interpretable |
| **Mahalanobis Distance (RMD)** | Distance | Yes | Yes | Low | Good for OOD/near-OOD | Needs feature statistics; assumes Gaussian |
| **Cosine Similarity to Nearest Neighbor** | Distance | Yes | Yes | Low | Captures typicality | Needs representative calibration set |
| **MC-Dropout (if applicable)** | Bayesian approx. | Yes | No (multi-pass) | High | Principled epistemic uncertainty | Requires dropout; slow |
| **Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)** | Augmentation | Yes | No (multi-pass) | Medium | Robustness to image perturbations | Only vision-side; slow |
| **Semantic Entropy** | Sampling | Yes | No (multi-sample) | High | Best for hallucination detection | Expensive; requires entailment model |
| **LN-Entropy / Perplexity** | Output | Yes | Yes | Free | Simple for sequences | Confounded by length and token frequency |
| **Hidden-State Probe (e.g., SAPLMA)** | Probing | No (probe training) | Yes | Low | Can detect internal misalignment | Needs labeled data; may not generalize across shifts |
| **p(True) / Verbalized Confidence** | Verbalized | Yes | Yes | Minimal | Zero architectural access | Poorly calibrated; overconfident |

**Key Insight:** The proposed cross-modal KL divergence occupies a unique position: it is **training-free** (no VLM fine-tuning), **single-pass** (one forward pass per modality), and **feature-based** (not score-based). Its primary differentiation from the baselines above is that it measures **cross-modal alignment** rather than **output confidence** or **input typicality**. This may allow it to detect failures where the model is confident but the vision and text representations are inconsistent — a failure mode that entropy, MSP, and even calibration methods cannot capture.

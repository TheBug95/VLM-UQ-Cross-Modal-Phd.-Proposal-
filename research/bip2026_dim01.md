## 1. Paper Overview & Core Contribution

**Claim:** The paper proposes a compact, per-instance uncertainty estimation (UE) method for LLMs that scores cross-layer agreement patterns in internal representations using a single forward pass. It matches probing in-distribution and outperforms it under distribution shift and 4-bit quantization. [^1]

**Source:** arXiv:2603.22299v1
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22299v1
**Date:** 2026-03-17
**Excerpt:**
> "We propose a compact, per-instance UE method that scores cross-layer agreement patterns in internal representations using a single forward pass. Across three models, our method matches probing in-distribution, with mean diagonal differences of at most -1.8 AUPRC percentage points and +4.9 Brier score points. Under cross-dataset transfer, it consistently outperforms probing, achieving off-diagonal gains up to +2.86 AUPRC and +21.02 Brier points."

**Context:** The method is post-hoc, training-free (for inference), and requires only a single forward pass. It is model-agnostic and introduces no architectural modifications. The key insight is that structured cross-layer divergence patterns preserve transferable dynamics that are more robust than raw activations.
**Confidence:** high

## 2. Key Equations & Methodology

### 2.1 Post-MLP Activations as Probability Distributions

For each layer ℓ and task-relevant token t, the post-MLP activation vector is converted to a probability distribution via temperature-scaled softmax over the hidden dimension:

$$p_\ell^{(t)} = \text{softmax}(h_\ell^{(t)} / \tau)$$

Where $h_\ell^{(t)}$ is the hidden state at layer ℓ for token t, and τ is a temperature hyperparameter.

### 2.2 Pairwise Directed KL Divergence (Signature Map)

The directed divergence matrix $S^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$ is defined as:

$$S_{ij}^{(t)} = D_{KL}\big(p_i^{(t)} \,\|\, p_j^{(t)}\big), \qquad i,j \in \{1,\ldots,L\}$$

Where $D_{KL}$ is the KL divergence. The paper also supports the symmetric **Jensen-Shannon divergence**:

$$D_{JS} := \frac{1}{2} D_{KL}(p \| m) + \frac{1}{2} D_{KL}(q \| m), \quad \text{where } m := \frac{p+q}{2}$$

### 2.3 Contrast Transformation (Optional)

After constructing the divergence matrix, an element-wise contrast transformation is applied to improve uniformity and dynamic range:

$$S'^{(t)} = 1 - \exp(-\alpha S^{(t)}), \quad \alpha > 0$$

Where α is a tunable hyperparameter.

### 2.4 Feature Flattening

The signature map is flattened into a feature vector:

$$z^{(t)} = \text{Flatten}(S'^{(t)})$$

This yields a compact representation of size $L^2$, which is typically much smaller than the raw hidden dimension $d_{hidden}$ (e.g., $L=32 \Rightarrow L^2=1024$ vs $d_{hidden}=4096$).

### 2.5 Uncertainty Estimation via Gradient Boosting

A LightGBM classifier is trained per task on $z^{(t)}$ to predict correctness. The uncertainty score is:

$$\hat{u}(z^{(t)}) = 1 - P(\text{correct} \mid z^{(t)})$$

Raw probabilities are reported without post-hoc calibration (though Platt scaling or isotonic regression could be applied).

**Source:** arXiv:2603.22299v1, Sections 3.3–3.4
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22299v1
**Date:** 2026-03-17
**Confidence:** high

## 3. Algorithmic Steps (Step-by-Step)

**Step 1: Single Forward Pass**
- Run the LLM on input x with `output_hidden_states=True`.
- Collect hidden states for all L layers at task-relevant token positions.

**Step 2: Token Selection**
- For QA: either the final generated token or exact answer tokens (obtained via auxiliary procedure).
- For classification: the predicted class token.
- Strategy follows Orgad et al. (2025).

**Step 3: Distribution Conversion**
- For each layer ℓ and selected token t, apply temperature-scaled softmax over the hidden dimension:
  $$p_\ell^{(t)} = \text{softmax}(h_\ell^{(t)} / \tau)$$

**Step 4: Pairwise KL Divergence**
- Compute the L×L matrix $S^{(t)}$ where $S_{ij}^{(t)} = D_{KL}(p_i^{(t)} \| p_j^{(t)})$.
- Optional: use Jensen-Shannon divergence instead of KL for symmetry.

**Step 5: Contrast Transformation (Optional)**
- Apply $S'^{(t)} = 1 - \exp(-\alpha S^{(t)})$ to improve dynamic range.

**Step 6: Flatten to Features**
- $z^{(t)} = \text{Flatten}(S'^{(t)})$ → vector of length $L^2$.

**Step 7: Train LightGBM Classifier**
- Train a gradient-boosted decision tree (LightGBM) on $\{z^{(t)}, y^{(t)}\}$ where $y^{(t)} \in \{0,1\}$ is correctness.
- Per-task training (one classifier per dataset/task).

**Step 8: Inference-Time Uncertainty Scoring**
- For a new input, compute $z^{(t)}$ and feed to the trained LightGBM.
- Output: $\hat{u} = 1 - P(\text{correct} \mid z^{(t)})$.

The per-task training means the method is not fully training-free overall (it requires a small labeled dataset to train the GBDT), but the LLM itself is never modified or fine-tuned. ## 5. Comparison to Related Methods

### 5.1 Probing (Azaria & Mitchell 2023; Orgad et al. 2025)

**Claim:** Probing methods train classifiers directly on high-dimensional hidden states ($d_{hidden}$). They are effective but task-specific and difficult to interpret or transfer. The signature map approach compresses the signal into $L^2$ features, making it more compact and transferable. [^1]

**Source:** arXiv:2603.22299v1, Section 2.1
**Confidence:** high

### 5.2 LOS-NET & ACT-ViT (Bar-Shalom et al., 2025a, 2025b)

**Claim:** LOS-NET models the full sequence of next-token probability distributions (output-level). ACT-ViT uses activation-based methods. Both are compared in the paper's appendix. Signature maps outperform both in challenging reasoning-heavy settings (e.g., HotpotQA +13.83 pp over LOS-NET). [^1]

**Source:** arXiv:2603.22299v1, Appendix D
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22299v1
**Date:** 2026-03-17
**Excerpt:**
> "When trained and evaluated on HotpotQA, Signatures outperforms both LOS-NET (+13.83 pp) and ACT-ViT (+18.45 pp), indicating that it captures task-relevant structure more effectively under aligned supervision."

**Confidence:** high

### 5.3 DoLa (Chuang et al., 2023) & Uncertainty-Aware Decoding

**Claim:** DoLa and similar methods modify the decoding process rather than estimating reliability post-hoc. They alter generation dynamics but do not produce per-instance uncertainty estimates. The signature method is complementary: it produces a calibrated correctness probability that can trigger DoLa or selective generation. [^1]

**Source:** arXiv:2603.22299v1, Section 2.5
**Confidence:** high

### 5.4 Information Bottleneck (Tishby et al., 1999)

**Claim:** The method sits between classic probing and IB analysis. IB requires population-level mutual information estimates ($I(X;T)$) that are impractical at inference time. Signature maps use local, per-instance, information-theoretic structure without global distribution estimation. [^1]

**Source:** arXiv:2603.22299v1, Sections 2.4 & 6
**Confidence:** high

## 7. Pitfalls for VLM Adaptation

### 7.1 From Unimodal LLM to Cross-Modal VLM

The original paper operates on a single sequence of hidden states from an LLM (all layers share the same hidden dimension and token structure). For a VLM like MedGemma:

- **Vision branch:** Produces patch tokens from the vision encoder (e.g., 256 tokens of dimension 1152 from MedSigLIP).
- **Text branch:** Produces token embeddings from the language decoder (e.g., sequence of dimension 2560).
- **Different dimensionalities:** Raw vision hidden states (1152-dim) vs. text hidden states (2560-dim) cannot be directly compared without projection.
- **Different token semantics:** Vision tokens correspond to spatial patches; text tokens correspond to linguistic units. Their distributions are semantically different.

### 7.2 Recommended Adaptation Strategy

**Option A (Projected Vision Tokens):**
- Use the projected vision tokens from the decoder stack (after the multi-modal projector). These already live in the 2560-dim space and can be directly compared with text hidden states.
- Compute KL between mean-pooled vision representation and mean-pooled text representation.

**Option B (Vocabulary-Space Projection):**
- Project both vision and text hidden states to the vocabulary space using the language model's unembedding matrix.
- Compute KL over the vocabulary distributions.
- This ensures both distributions are over the same support (vocabulary size).

**Option C (Gaussian Approximation in Hidden Space):**
- Treat each hidden state as a Gaussian distribution (mean = hidden state, variance = fixed or learned).
- Compute KL between Gaussians in the shared hidden space (requires dimensionality alignment first).

### 7.3 Asymmetry of KL

KL divergence is asymmetric: $D_{KL}(P \| Q) \neq D_{KL}(Q \| P)$. For cross-modal uncertainty, both directions may be informative:
- $D_{KL}(\text{vision} \| \text{text})$: Does the image "explain" more than the text captures?
- $D_{KL}(\text{text} \| \text{vision})$: Does the text "explain" more than the image shows?
- High values in either direction indicate misalignment.

The paper supports Jensen-Shannon divergence as a symmetric alternative.

### 7.4 Modality Gap

Even for aligned image-text pairs, CLIP/SigLIP models exhibit a systematic "modality gap" where vision and text embeddings form separate clusters. This means non-zero KL divergence may be normal even for correct predictions. The uncertainty signal must be calibrated against this baseline (e.g., by learning a threshold or classifier on the divergence values, as the paper does with LightGBM).

## 8. Interpretability & Mechanistic Analysis

**Claim:** Signature maps provide a practical bridge between black-box uncertainty estimation and mechanistic interpretability. Feature importance from LightGBM reveals which layer interactions most strongly influence correctness predictions. Different models show qualitatively different attribution profiles (e.g., Mistral shows monotonic decay with inter-layer distance; Qwen shows sparse long-range interactions). [^1]

**Source:** arXiv:2603.22299v1, Appendix B & E
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22299v1
**Date:** 2026-03-17
**Excerpt:**
> "For Mistral, importance decays monotonically with inter-layer distance, whereas Qwen shows a sparser pattern with influential interactions persisting across longer separations. We hypothesize that these differences reflect architectural or training-induced inductive biases."

**Context:** For a VLM adaptation, cross-modal divergence maps could similarly reveal whether misalignment occurs primarily in early layers (vision encoding) or late layers (text generation), offering diagnostic value.
**Confidence:** high

## 9. Code & Implementation Notes

### 9.1 Code Availability

**Claim:** As of June 2026, no official open-source code repository for the paper has been identified. The paper is very recent (March 2026). Reimplementation from the equations in Section 3 is straightforward. [^1]

**Context:** Key implementation components needed:
1. Extract hidden states: `model(..., output_hidden_states=True)` in transformers.
2. Temperature-scaled softmax over hidden dim.
3. Pairwise KL divergence computation (vectorized for efficiency).
4. Optional contrast transformation.
5. Flatten and train LightGBM (`lightgbm` Python package).

### 9.2 Pseudocode for Signature Map Computation

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F
import lightgbm as lgb
import numpy as np

def compute_signature_map(hidden_states, token_idx=-1, tau=1.0, alpha=1.0, use_js=False):
    """
    hidden_states: tuple of L tensors, each [batch, seq_len, hidden_dim]
    token_idx: which token to analyze (default: last token)
    Returns: signature map S' of shape [batch, L, L]
    """
    L = len(hidden_states)
    batch_size = hidden_states[0].shape[0]
    
    # Step 1: Extract token hidden states
    h = torch.stack([hs[:, token_idx, :] for hs in hidden_states], dim=1)  # [batch, L, hidden_dim]
    
    # Step 2: Temperature-scaled softmax over hidden dimension
    p = F.softmax(h / tau, dim=-1)  # [batch, L, hidden_dim]
    
    # Step 3: Pairwise KL divergence
    # For efficiency, use broadcasting: KL(P_i || P_j) = sum(P_i * (log P_i - log P_j))
    log_p = torch.log(p + 1e-12)  # [batch, L, hidden_dim]
    
    # Expand for pairwise: [batch, L, 1, hidden_dim] vs [batch, 1, L, hidden_dim]
    p_i = p.unsqueeze(2)          # [batch, L, 1, hidden_dim]
    log_p_i = log_p.unsqueeze(2)  # [batch, L, 1, hidden_dim]
    log_p_j = log_p.unsqueeze(1)  # [batch, 1, L, hidden_dim]
    
    S = (p_i * (log_p_i - log_p_j)).sum(dim=-1)  # [batch, L, L]
    
    if use_js:
        # Jensen-Shannon: symmetric
        m = 0.5 * (p_i + p.unsqueeze(1))  # [batch, L, L, hidden_dim]
        log_m = torch.log(m + 1e-12)
        S = 0.5 * ((p_i * (log_p_i - log_m)).sum(dim=-1) + 
                   (p.unsqueeze(1) * (log_p.unsqueeze(1) - log_m)).sum(dim=-1))
    
    # Step 4: Contrast transformation
    S_prime = 1 - torch.exp(-alpha * S)
    
    return S_prime

# Training the LightGBM classifier
X_train = np.array([S_prime.flatten() for S_prime in signature_maps_train])  # [N, L*L]
y_train = np.array(correctness_labels)  # 1 if correct, 0 if incorrect

    'metric': 'binary_logloss',
    'boosting_type': 'gbdt',
    'num_leaves': 31,
    'learning_rate': 0.05,
    'feature_fraction': 0.9,
    'bagging_fraction': 0.8,
    'bagging_freq': 5,
    'verbose': -1
}
```

## 10. Synthesis for BIP 2026 Proposal

### Adaptation Path: From Intra-Layer to Cross-Modal

| Component | Original (LLM) | Adaptation (VLM) |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|
| Hidden states | L layers, same dim | Vision branch + Text branch, different dims |
| Distribution | Softmax over hidden dim | Same, but need common support |
| Pairwise KL | L×L layer-layer matrix | 1×1 cross-modal KL (or expanded) |
| Token focus | Answer / last token | Answer token(s) in text branch |
| Classifier | LightGBM on L² features | LightGBM on divergence features + metadata |
| Interpretability | Layer interaction patterns | Vision-text alignment patterns |

### Recommended Cross-Modal Signature Design

For the BIP 2026 proposal, we recommend:

1. **Extract projected vision tokens** from the decoder hidden states (positions 0:256, dim=2560).
2. **Extract text tokens** from the decoder hidden states (positions 256:, dim=2560).
3. **Mean-pool** both to get single vectors: $h_{vis}, h_{txt} \in \mathbb{R}^{2560}$.
4. **Apply softmax** over the 2560-dim feature space to get $p_{vis}, p_{txt}$.
5. **Compute KL divergence** $D_{KL}(p_{vis} \| p_{txt})$ and optionally $D_{KL}(p_{txt} \| p_{vis})$.
6. **Add auxiliary features:** token entropy, answer probability, modality gap baseline.
7. **Train LightGBM** on a small validation set to predict correctness from these features.
8. **Report:** AUPRC, Brier score, t-test for significance.

This design preserves the paper's core principles (single-pass, structured divergence, lightweight classifier) while adapting to the cross-modal setting of MedGemma + fundus images.

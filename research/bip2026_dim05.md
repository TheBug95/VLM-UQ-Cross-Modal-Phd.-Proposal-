### Claim 1.3: Image-level labeling (derived from diagnostic keywords) can improve performance over naive patient-level splitting by reducing label noise.

## 2. Metrics Debate: AUPRC vs. AUROC for Imbalanced Medical Screening

### Claim 2.1: The widespread belief that AUPRC is superior to AUROC under class imbalance is being challenged by recent rigorous theoretical and empirical work.
- **Source:** McDermott et al. (NeurIPS 2024) — "A Closer Look at AUROC and AUPRC under Class Imbalance"
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.06091v2.pdf; https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/95133
- **Date:** 2024-01-11; NeurIPS 2024
- **Excerpt:** "Our empirical investigations highlight the robustness of AUROC to class imbalance, which may be desirable especially in domains like healthcare. In contrast, we find that selecting models using overall AUPRC can lead to disparities across subpopulations... AUPRC is suited for single-group retrieval, not class-imbalanced binary classification."
- **Context:** The paper reviewed 1.5M papers and found many misattributed citations. The key insight: AUROC treats all false positives equally; AUPRC weights them by inverse firing rate, which can amplify bias against low-prevalence subgroups.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^6]

### Claim 2.2: AUROC is robust to class imbalance and provides a more generalizable, unbiased assessment; AUPRC is appropriate for retrieval tasks (top-k) but may be inappropriate for diagnostic predictions where each patient is evaluated individually.
- **Source:** Richardson et al. / McDermott et al. (2024)
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1617142/pdf
- **Date:** 2025
- **Excerpt:** "AUC-ROC provides a general estimate of classifier performance, while AUPRC reflects the classifier's performance on a specific dataset... Reference Richardson et al. (2024) recommends using AUC-ROC alongside AUPRC and AP@50, as it enables fairer comparisons of models across datasets with varying class distributions."
- **Context:** For our ODIR-5K glaucoma task (6% prevalence), we should report both AUROC and AUPRC, but interpret AUROC as the primary model-ranking metric and AUPRC as a secondary measure of precision at operational thresholds.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^7]

### Claim 2.3: In clinical contexts with rare events, AUPRC can still be valuable because it focuses on precision-recall tradeoffs that are operationally relevant, though it depends heavily on prevalence.
- **Source:** Martin et al. (2025) / Frontiers in Pharmacology review
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1617142/pdf
- **Date:** 2025
- **Excerpt:** "In highly imbalanced datasets, such as those involving critical illness events, AUROC can be misleadingly high due to its dependence on true negative rates... AUPRC focuses on the tradeoff between precision and recall, which are more operationally relevant in clinical decision-making."
- **Context:** For glaucoma screening on ODIR-5K, we will report both metrics. AUPRC helps us understand precision at high-sensitivity operating points, while AUROC provides a threshold-independent summary of discriminative ability.
- **Confidence:** High [^7]

## 3. Brier Score in Medical AI: Calibrated Probability Estimation

### Claim 3.1: The Brier score is increasingly used in medical research to evaluate the validity of probabilistic predictions and measures calibration quality via mean squared error between predicted probabilities and actual outcomes.
- **Source:** Raji (2023) / Vijayan et al. (various)
- **URL:** https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/131022/RajiWareez.pdf
- **Date:** 2023
- **Excerpt:** "The Brier score was a measure developed to measure the accuracy of weather forecasts based on Euclidean distance between the actual outcome and the predicted probability... It is growingly common to use the Brier score in medical research to evaluate and compare the validity of binary predictions or prediction models."
- **Context:** For our zero-shot MedGemma 4B, the Brier score will tell us whether the model's output probabilities (from cosine similarity or logit scores) are well-calibrated or systematically over/under-confident. A lower Brier score = better calibration.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^8]

### Claim 3.2: Confidence-Calibrated Label Smoothing (CC-LS) loss achieves Brier score 0.098 on glaucoma classification, significantly outperforming BCE (0.195) and Focal loss (0.145), demonstrating that calibration-specific loss functions can dramatically improve probability reliability in ophthalmology.
- **Source:** Advancing Glaucoma Diagnosis (2024) — "Employing Confidence-Calibrated Label Smoothing Loss for Model Calibration"
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11381854/
- **Date:** 2024-11-27
- **Excerpt:** "The calculated Brier Score Loss values... BCE: 0.195, Focal loss: 0.145, CC-LS loss: 0.098... models employing the CC-LS mechanism exhibit superior calibration metrics, as evidenced by a Brier score of 0.098, along with notable accuracy measures: sensitivity of 81%, specificity of 80%."
- **Context:** Our training-free method cannot use CC-LS loss (requires training), but this establishes the Brier score as a standard calibration metric for glaucoma detection. We can apply temperature scaling post-hoc to improve calibration of our zero-shot model.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^9]

## 4. Class Imbalance in Ophthalmology: Techniques and Challenges

### Claim 4.1: Cost-sensitive learning, threshold adjustment, and hybrid loss functions are the dominant algorithmic approaches for handling class imbalance in medical imaging, with thresholding being the most accessible post-hoc method.
- **Source:** Johnson & Khoshgoftaar (2019) / Fernández et al. (2018) / Sheng & Ling (2006) — reviewed in "Cost-sensitive learning for imbalanced medical data: a review" (2024)
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-023-10652-8
- **Date:** 2024-03-01
- **Excerpt:** "Thresholding operates by minimising the expected cost based on the specified cost matrix... employs the threshold p* to adapt the decision threshold (usually set at 0.5) when categorising samples in a way that reduces bias towards the majority class."
- **Context:** For our zero-shot MedGemma, we cannot modify the training loss (no training), but we can use threshold tuning on a validation set to optimize sensitivity/specificity for glaucoma. This is a critical post-processing step.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^10]

### Claim 4.2: SMOTE and oversampling are common but introduce risks of false correlations and ethical concerns in medical data; they are less suitable for small datasets with extreme imbalance (>1:100).
- **Source:** Zhou et al. (2026) — "Strategies for Class-Imbalanced Learning in Multi-Sensor Medical Imaging"
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13029843/
- **Date:** 2026-01-03
- **Excerpt:** "Synthetic samples may introduce false correlations, especially in small-sample or high-dimensional data, potentially violating medical logic and posing ethical risks... In extreme imbalance scenarios (imbalance ratio > 1:100), it is necessary to combine with threshold adjustment of logistic regression, cost-sensitive learning, or enhanced sampling strategies."
- **Context:** ODIR-5K glaucoma ratio is ~1:16 (207 positive vs ~3,300 negative), which is imbalanced but not extreme. SMOTE could be used for training-based baselines, but our zero-shot approach does not require it.
- **Confidence:** High [^11]

### Claim 4.3: In ODIR-5K specifically, class imbalance causes low sensitivity for minority diseases (glaucoma, hypertension) even when overall AUC is high.
- **Source:** Lu et al. (2023) — "Automatic Multilabel Classification of Multiple Fundus Diseases" (PMC)
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9872849/
- **Date:** 2023
- **Excerpt:** "The ODIR database has serious class imbalances... the low sensitivity in the case of high specificity is because, for that particular class, the number of negative images is much higher than positive images... local features are not obvious. The determination of glaucoma requires an accurate ratio of the optic cup and disc."
- **Context:** This confirms that glaucoma is hard to detect on ODIR-5K not just due to imbalance but also because the relevant features (optic cup-to-disc ratio) are subtle and require precise localization.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^12]

## 6. Patient-Level vs. Image-Level Evaluation

### Claim 6.1: ODIR-5K labels are per-patient, but splitting both eyes into independent samples introduces label noise when disease is unilateral.
- **Source:** StackOverflow discussion (2026) — "ODIR-5K: Should I train a Dual-Input CNN or split images?"
- **URL:** https://stackoverflow.com/questions/79885777/odir-5k-should-i-train-a-dual-input-cnn-left-right-or-split-images-for-patien
- **Date:** 2026-03-06
- **Excerpt:** "Approach 1 (Single-Input): This introduces 'Label Noise.' If a patient has a Cataract only in the Left eye, splitting the data forces the model to treat the healthy Right eye as 'Cataract Positive.'... Approach 2 (Dual-Input): Keep the patient grouped and feed both eyes simultaneously."
- **Context:** For our zero-shot evaluation, we should evaluate at the patient level (aggregating both eyes) to match the ground truth labels, or explicitly note if we evaluate per-image. The GitHub repo by JosueVP17 uses patient-level 5-fold splits to prevent leakage.
- **Confidence:** High (practitioner consensus) [^15]

### Claim 6.2: Some recent works derive image-level labels from diagnostic keywords to enable per-eye evaluation, improving model training but requiring keyword availability.
- **Source:** Sivaz et al. (2025) — same as Claim 1.3
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0045790625003532
- **Date:** 2025-05-01
- **Excerpt:** "This study obtained image-level label information via the diagnostic keywords assigned by experts for each image in the OIA-ODIR dataset... Thus, it became possible to classify retinal diseases at the image-level instead of the patient-level."

- **Confidence:** High [^3]

## 10. Statistical Testing: AUC Comparison, Bootstrapping, and Confidence Intervals

### Claim 10.1: DeLong's test is the recommended method for comparing two correlated ROC curves (same test set), providing p-values and confidence intervals for AUC differences.
- **Source:** MedCalc Manual; pROC R package documentation; MLstatkit
- **URL:** https://www.medcalc.org/en/manual/comparison-of-roc-curves.php; https://github.com/Brritany/MLstatkit
- **Date:** 2026-06-22; 2024-03-29
- **Excerpt:** "Use the method of DeLong et al. (1988) for the calculation of the Standard Error of the Area Under the Curve (AUC) and of the difference between two AUCs (recommended)." / "DeLong's test for comparing the AUCs of two correlated ROC curves."
- **Context:** For our BIP, we should use DeLong's test to compare our zero-shot MedGemma + uncertainty vs. baseline zero-shot MedGemma on the same test set. MLstatkit (Python) provides an implementation.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^26]

### Claim 10.2: For AUPRC comparisons, paired stratified bootstrap with 1,000 iterations is the standard approach, as DeLong's test does not apply to PR curves. Holm correction should be used for multiple comparisons.
- **Source:** arXiv (2026) — multiple papers; SiamAF paper (2023)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12450; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09203
- **Date:** 2026; 2023
- **Excerpt:** "For AUPRC, we use paired, stratified bootstrap to form CIs and p-values. We adjust for multiple comparisons using Holm... We conducted bootstrapping tests with N=1000 samples to calculate the confidence interval for statistical significance comparisons."
- **Context:** We will report 95% CIs for AUPRC via stratified bootstrap (1,000 iterations) and use Holm correction if comparing multiple uncertainty methods.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^27]

### Claim 10.3: Bootstrapping by patient (with replacement) is critical for medical datasets to preserve patient-level correlation structure; simply bootstrapping by image can inflate statistical significance.
- **Source:** SiamAF (2023) — "SIAMAF: Learning Shared Information from ECG and PPG Signals"
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09203
- **Date:** 2023
- **Excerpt:** "Each bootstrap sample is constructed by randomly sampling patients with replacements from each test set... For each bootstrap sample, AUROC and AUPRC scores were calculated and collected. We calculated the 95% confidence interval using μ ± (1.96)σ/√N."
- **Context:** ODIR-5K has patient-level labels with two eyes per patient. We must bootstrap at the patient level, not the image level, to avoid violating independence assumptions.

## 11. Cohen's Kappa in Medical AI Evaluation

### Claim 11.1: Cohen's Kappa is widely used for inter-rater agreement in medical imaging; values >0.6 are considered substantial, and >0.8 almost perfect. However, Kappa below 0.60 indicates inadequate agreement.
- **Source:** McHugh (2012) — "Interrater reliability: the kappa statistic" (PMC, cited 26,058 times)
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3900052/
- **Date:** 2012 (foundational)
- **Excerpt:** "Cohen suggested: 0.01–0.20 none to slight, 0.21–0.40 fair, 0.41–0.60 moderate, 0.61–0.80 substantial, 0.81–1.00 almost perfect... any kappa below 0.60 indicates inadequate agreement among the raters and little confidence should be placed in the study results."
- **Context:** The ODIR Challenge uses Kappa as one of its official metrics (along with AUC and F1). We should report Kappa for our zero-shot classification to align with the challenge protocol. A Kappa >0.4 for glaucoma would be considered acceptable given the difficulty.
- **Confidence:** Very High [^30]

### Claim 11.2: ODIR-5K challenge evaluation uses the arithmetic mean of F1, AUC, and Cohen's Kappa as the "Final Score".
- **Source:** Czech Technical University thesis (2024) — "Medical Multimodal Classification for Fundus-Detectable Diseases"
- **URL:** https://dspace.cuni.cz/bitstream/handle/20.500.11956/207010/120534995.pdf
- **Date:** 2024
- **Excerpt:** "Evaluation follows the ODIR Challenge metrics — F1, AUC, Cohen's Kappa — and their 'Final score' (the arithmetic mean of these metrics)."
- **Context:** To be comparable with ODIR literature, we should compute F1, AUC, and Kappa for glaucoma, then report the Final Score = (F1 + AUC + Kappa) / 3.
- **Confidence:** High [^31]

## 12. Cross-Modal KL Divergence as an Uncertainty Signal

### Claim 12.1: KL divergence between multi-scale predictions has been used for uncertainty estimation in medical image segmentation, requiring only a single forward pass (unlike Monte Carlo dropout).
- **Source:** Huang et al. (2024) — "A review of uncertainty quantification in medical image analysis" (cited 152 times)
- **URL:** https://hal.science/hal-04445569v2/file/MIA2023_june_arxiv.pdf
- **Date:** 2024
- **Excerpt:** "Uncertainty is estimated via the KL-divergence among multi-scale predictions, which only need a single forward pass compared with MCD."
- **Context:** Our proposed method uses cross-modal KL divergence (between image and text embeddings) rather than multi-scale intra-modal KL, but the principle is analogous: disagreement between parallel representations signals uncertainty.
- **Confidence:** High [^32]

### Claim 12.2: KL divergence is a standard component of variational inference and uncertainty quantification in medical imaging, used to measure divergence between approximate and true posterior distributions.
- **Source:** Barbano (2022) — "Uncertainty quantification in medical image synthesis" (cited 37 times)
- **URL:** https://rt416.github.io/pdf/image_synthesis_chapter.pdf
- **Date:** 2022
- **Excerpt:** "VI is often based on approximately minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the target distribution and the approximate surrogate one. The divergence KL from q to p is defined by KL(q||p) = ∫ q(x) log(q(x)/p(x)) dx."
- **Context:** Our training-free method uses KL divergence between the image embedding distribution and text embedding distribution as an uncertainty proxy. This is computationally efficient and does not require model modification.
- **Confidence:** High [^33]

## 13. Synthesis: Key Takeaways for the BIP 2026 Proposal

### 13.1 Evaluation Protocol Checklist
- **Primary metrics:** AUROC (threshold-independent), AUPRC (precision at operational thresholds), F1-score (balanced metric), Cohen's Kappa (inter-rater agreement proxy).
- **Calibration metrics:** Brier score, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), reliability diagrams.
- **Uncertainty metrics:** Correlation between KL-divergence and misclassification, coverage of high-uncertainty cases, accuracy improvement after excluding top-K% uncertain cases.
- **Statistical tests:** DeLong's test for AUROC comparisons; paired stratified bootstrap (1,000 iterations, patient-level) for AUPRC CIs; McNemar's test for accuracy; Holm correction for multiple comparisons.
- **Quality stratification:** Report metrics by image quality (good/usable/reject) to show uncertainty-quality correlation.

### 13.2 Clinical Relevance
- Target sensitivity >80% for screening (matching SD-OCT performance).
- Use uncertainty thresholds to triage: auto-decision (low uncertainty), clinician-assist (medium), manual review (high).
- Calibration is essential for clinical trust; poorly calibrated probabilities lead to dangerous overconfidence.

### 13.3 Research Gap
- No prior work has evaluated **training-free cross-modal uncertainty estimation** (KL divergence between image and text embeddings) for zero-shot glaucoma detection on ODIR-5K with comprehensive medical metrics (AUPRC, Brier, Kappa, ECE) and patient-level bootstrapped CIs.
- Prior VLM work on fundus images focuses on classification accuracy, not uncertainty calibration or quality-aware triage.
